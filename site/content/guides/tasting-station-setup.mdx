You don't need a bar cart or a dedicated room. You need a flat surface, good light, and a handful of things within arm's reach so nobody has to get up mid-pairing.

## The checklist

- **One glass per guest per spirit**, or a water rinse between pours if glasses are tight. Rocks glasses work, but a plain wine glass concentrates aroma better if you have them.
- **Water pitcher and small cups.** Non-negotiable. Water resets the palate between pairings and keeps a high-proof night from catching up with anyone too fast.
- **Plain crackers or bread.** A neutral palate cleanser that doesn't fight the next pairing the way flavored crackers or salty snacks do.
- **A pen at every seat.** The tasting cards in every kit ask for notes in the moment, not from memory afterward.
- **Good light, not mood light.** You can dim it for the vibe once everyone's poured, but reading a flavor wheel by candlelight is a real problem. Set up under real light, then adjust.
- **Labels or a seating chart**, especially for a blind round. If you're pouring more than one bottle per tier, mark the bottles' bottoms, not their labels, so guests can't peek.

## Lay it out so people don't have to get up

Put the flavor wheel, the shopping list's leftover reference, and the tasting cards for the current round in the middle of the table, not off to the side. The entire point of a printed kit is that nobody has to look anything up on their phone mid-pour. If the reference material is out of reach, people default to their phones anyway, and the phone always wins.

## What to skip

Skip the fancy decanting unless the spirit genuinely benefits from it (some aged whiskies do; most tequila and mezcal don't). Skip scented candles near the tasting area entirely. Skip serving food that competes with the pairing itself, save the real meal for after the cards are filled in.

Thirty minutes, one trip to set the table, and you're ready before the first guest arrives.

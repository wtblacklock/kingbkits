Rum & Tiki Night

Sip it slow, then build something fun with it
KINGBKITS

PRE-PARTY PLANNING

  • Two pours per guest per rum - one neat, one built into a tiki drink. Three rums covers a full flight.
  • Pick one tier from the Rumbase and stay in it - mixing a $18 rum against a $45 pot-still rum just makes the cheap one taste flat.
  • Buying or BYO both work - three bottles covers the tasting; extras only matter for a bigger crowd.

SET A DATE AND SEND INVITES

  • Say "tiki night," not "rum tasting." It sets the right expectation: playful, not a spirits seminar.
  • Tell guests to eat first. Tiki drinks hide their proof behind citrus and syrup.

PARTY SETUP

  • Crushed ice matters more here than any spirit in this line - see the Tiki Primer before you buy.
  • Set out citrus, garnishes, and glassware for a second round.
  • A tasting card and a small glass at every seat before anyone pours.

DURING THE PARTY

  • Taste each rum neat first, then on the rocks, then in the tiki drink - the same rum reads differently each way.
  • Rotate through the tier lightest to boldest, same rule as every kit in this line.
  • Close with a vote on the night's best build, not just the best rum.

SNACK PAIRINGS

Grilled pineapple, coconut shrimp, spiced nuts, dark chocolate. Skip heavy cream sauces - they flatten the citrus in every drink that follows.

Primer

Know Your Rum, Build Your Drink

Rum has no legal style rulebook - here's the one that actually matters at the bar.
KINGBKITS

Rum Styles

Unlike Scotch or tequila, "rum" has almost no enforced production rules - style is set by habit and geography, not law.
White / Silver

Light-bodied, often filtered after aging to strip color. Clean, faintly sweet - the Newbie-tier default and the tiki backbone for a Daiquiri.

Gold / Amber

Aged longer or colored for a mixing-friendly middle ground. More vanilla and caramel than white, still light enough to mix.

Dark / Navy Strength

Pot-still and pit-aged, often blended from multiple islands. Funky esters, char, dried fruit - what a proper Jungle Bird or Navy Grog is built on.

Agricole & Rhum

Distilled from fresh cane juice instead of molasses, mostly French Caribbean. Grassy, vegetal, almost tequila-adjacent - a different animal entirely.

The Tiki Formula

Every classic tiki drink is the same four-part structure, just with different rums and fruit in each slot.
Spirit

Usually two rums of different styles layered together - that's what "tiki" actually means more than any single ingredient.

Sour

Fresh lime almost always, sometimes with orange or passionfruit alongside it. Bottled juice is the single most common way to ruin a tiki drink.

Sweet

Orgeat (almond syrup), falernum, honey, or demerara syrup - picks the drink's whole personality more than the rum does.

Spice / Float

A dash of bitters, a float of overproof rum, or a spiced syrup on top - the finishing note, added last and never stirred in.

Cane to Cocktail

Milling

Sugarcane is crushed for juice, or the juice is boiled down into molasses first - this single fork decides agricole vs everything else.

Fermentation

Days for molasses-based rum, often under 48 hours for cane-juice agricole. Wild or added yeast, and it's a huge flavor lever either way.

Distillation

Column stills give a lighter, cleaner spirit. Pot stills keep more congeners and funk - Jamaican pot-still rum is the extreme end of this.

Aging

Tropical aging (Caribbean heat) matures rum roughly twice as fast as a cool warehouse - a 5-year tropical-aged rum can taste like a 10-year one elsewhere.

Blending

Most rum on a shelf is a blend of multiple ages and sometimes multiple islands - single-barrel rum is the exception, not the rule.

Glassware & Garnish Checklist
  • Tiki mugs or double rocks glasses - anything with some visual presence
  • Crushed ice, not cubed - it dilutes faster and that's the point in a tiki drink
  • Fresh limes, oranges - bottled juice will flatten every drink you make
  • Mint sprigs, pineapple fronds, or a paper umbrella for the theater of it
  • Orgeat or falernum if you're building real tiki drinks, not just pours

Checklist & Scorecard

8.5 × 11 in · 1 page
Piece 06

Shop Smart, Vote Fair

Everything on the list, then close the night with a real ballot.
KINGBKITS

SHOPPING CHECKLIST

  • Rum - one bottle per rum, your chosen tier (Rumbase, pg 3)
  • Fresh limes and oranges - not bottled juice
  • Orgeat or falernum, plus a demerara or simple syrup
  • Crushed ice - buy a bag or crush cubes yourself, don't skip this
  • Tiki mugs or double rocks glasses, one per guest
  • Mint, pineapple, or an umbrella for garnish
  • Angostura bitters
  • Pen at every seat for the Tasting Cards
Splitting what's left. Neat, rocks, and one small tiki drink per rum barely dents a 750ml bottle across six guests - expect real leftovers. Rum keeps indefinitely once opened, so the easiest move is letting the host hold onto it for the next round, or send a 1-2 oz sample per rum home in a small lidded jar.

Right glass for the table. A tiki mug or double rocks glass works for the whole tasting portion - no need for anything fancier until you're building drinks for a crowd.

TASTING NIGHT SCORECARD

Pass around at the end of the night. Whichever build gets the most votes is next round's starting point.
